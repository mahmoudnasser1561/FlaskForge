# Content Moderation Policy

Automated content moderation policy for StaffRoom, an internal team
discussion forum. Applies to the text of posts and comments.

Evaluate each category independently. A single clear match to any one
category below is sufficient to flag — content does not need to match
more than one. Judge the text as written; do not guess at the
author's intent. Do not exempt content because it is phrased
politely, framed as a joke, sarcasm, or opinion, or because it is
short or vague. If the text itself clearly matches a category, flag
it regardless of tone, framing, or how it is worded.

## Harassment and targeted insults
Flag: name-calling, insults, or demeaning language directed at a
named or clearly identifiable coworker; threats of violence or harm
against a person; organizing or encouraging others to target someone.

- Flag: "Alex is a lazy idiot and everyone should stop listening to
  him."
- Flag: "Someone should teach Jordan a lesson after that meeting."
- Do not flag: "I think Alex's proposal is poorly thought out and
  shouldn't move forward." — criticism of the work, not the person.

## Hate speech
Flag: content that attacks, demeans, or promotes discrimination
against people based on race, ethnicity, religion, national origin,
gender, sexual orientation, or disability.

- Flag: "People from [nationality/religion/ethnicity] shouldn't be
  trusted with this kind of work." — the pattern to catch is a
  categorical, demeaning claim about a protected group; the bracketed
  group is a placeholder, not a specific target.
- Flag: content asserting a group doesn't belong on the team because
  of who they are.
- Do not flag: neutral, factual mention of someone's background (e.g.
  "Priya is leading this from our Mumbai office").

## Sexual content
Flag: explicit sexual content, or content that sexualizes a specific
person.

- Flag: graphic sexual descriptions, or comments about a named
  coworker's body or appearance in a sexual way.
- Do not flag: incidental, non-explicit references made in a
  legitimate context (e.g. a benefits discussion mentioning parental
  leave).

## Promotion of illegal activity
Flag: instructions or encouragement to commit a crime — fraud, drug
distribution, unauthorized access to systems or accounts, or similar.

- Flag: content offering step-by-step help accessing someone else's
  account or system without authorization.
- Flag: content offering to sell stolen credentials or illegal
  substances.
- Do not flag: legitimate security discussion, e.g. explaining how a
  phishing email works so coworkers can recognize one.

## Spam and scam links
Flag: unsolicited advertising, repeated off-topic promotional
content, or links intended to phish credentials or defraud readers.

- Flag: "Click here to claim your free gift card: [link]" posted
  unprompted.
- Flag: the same promotional link posted repeatedly across unrelated
  threads.
- Do not flag: a coworker sharing a relevant tool or article once, on
  topic, with no ask for money or credentials.

## Dangerous misinformation
Flag: false claims presented as fact that could cause real harm if
acted on — for example false medical, safety, or emergency guidance.

- Flag: "You don't need to evacuate during a fire alarm, they're
  always fake."
- Flag: a false medical claim stated as settled fact rather than
  opinion.
- Do not flag: a hedge, question, or clearly personal opinion (e.g.
  "I'm not sure the fire drill schedule makes sense, has anyone
  checked?").

## Not a violation
Disagreement, blunt criticism of ideas or decisions, venting about
work, or off-topic posts that do not match any category above. Being
unpopular, rude, or low-effort is not by itself a violation — but
framing otherwise-qualifying content as a joke, sarcasm, or opinion
does not exempt it.

- Do not flag: "This meeting was a waste of time."
- Do not flag: "I disagree with this decision and think it will hurt
  the team long-term."
- Do not flag: an off-topic post about a personal hobby.
